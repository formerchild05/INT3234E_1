# CAMEF paper knowledge

## Scope and sources

- Updated: 2026-10-05.
- Current paper: `pp/2502.04592v3.pdf`.
- Title: CAMEF: Causal-Augmented Multi-Modality Event-Driven Financial Forecasting by Integrating Time Series Patterns and Salient Macroeconomic Announcements.
- Authors: Yang Zhang, Wenbo Yang, Jun Wang, Qiang Ma, Jie Xiong. KDD 2025; arXiv v3 dated 2025-08-08. DOI: 10.1145/3711896.3736872.
- Current code-review focus: map CAMEF Equations 7-8 to the repository multimodal fusion implementation.
- Primary PDF sections inspected: abstract/introduction p. 1; problem p. 3; data and counterfactuals pp. 4-5; architecture and loss pp. 5-7; experiments and ablations pp. 7-8; conclusion p. 9; Appendix C.2 p. 12.
- HTML cross-check: `https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04592v3`.

## Verified findings

- Task: use an announcement available at release time and the preceding financial time series to forecast the subsequent series. The model does not predict unreleased announcement content. [PDF Section 3, p. 3]
- Motivation: announcement text and historical market patterns contain complementary information. [PDF abstract and Sections 1-2]
- Dataset: six announcement types - FOMC, Unemployment Insurance Claims, Employment Situation, GDP Advance, CPI, and PPI - paired with SPX, INDU, NDX, USGG1M, and USGG5YR at 5-minute OHLC frequency. These are US stock indices and Treasury series, not individual stocks. Exact date ranges differ between the abstract, Section 4, and Table 1. [PDF Section 4.1, p. 4]
- Architecture: RoBERTa encodes text; MOMENT encodes historical series; projected representations are fused and passed through a GPT-2 decoder and post-regressor to produce numerical forecasts. GPT-2 is a network component rather than a price-answering chatbot. [PDF Sections 5.1-5.4]
- Counterfactual generation: LLaMA-3 8B summarizes announcements, assigns sentiment from 1 to 10, and modifies relevant numbers or phrases while keeping neutral context. The default set has 10 same-type variants and 5 nearby different-type announcements. [PDF Sections 4.2 and 5.5.1]
- Training combines MSE, MAE, and triplet loss. Triplet loss makes the factual announcement embedding closer to its paired time-series embedding than alternative announcements. Counterfactual worlds have no observed market outcomes as labels. [PDF Sections 5.4-5.5]
- Experiments use a 6:2:2 train/validation/test ratio. Horizons 35, 70, and 140 correspond to 175, 350, and 700 minutes. Section 6.1 does not say whether the split is chronological or random. Authors report first place in 24 of 30 asset/horizon/metric combinations; this is not 80 percent prediction accuracy.
- Example: SPX at horizon 35 has reported MSE 0.00048860 for CAMEF and 0.00073333 for TEST. Preprocessing, scaling, and reproduction remain unverified.

## Assessment and next checks

- Post-CAMEF literature scan (verified 2026-10-06; primary paper pages/official proceedings): no verified direct successor was found that reuses CAMEF's exact task, dataset, and LLM-counterfactual triplet-learning design. Closest finance-specific post-CAMEF work is Koval et al., *Multimodal Language Models with Modality-Specific Experts for Financial Forecasting from Interleaved Sequences of Text and Time Series* (arXiv:2509.19628, submitted 2025-09-23): it interleaves company news and daily returns, uses modality-specific experts plus SALMON alignment, and predicts 7/30-day stock direction; this is not macro-announcement high-frequency path regression. Broader post-CAMEF extensions include VoT, *Unlocking the Value of Text* (ICLR 2026/arXiv:2603.15452), which uses LLM event reasoning, historical in-context correction, endogenous-text alignment, and frequency-domain fusion across 10 domains; *From Text to Forecasts* (arXiv:2603.12664), which targets the text-to-quantitative temporal-evolution modality gap; Nexus (arXiv:2605.14389), an agentic context-aware forecasting framework; and ScenarioDiff (arXiv:2608.17164), which converts documents into scenarios and sparse forecast anchors for diffusion forecasting. Related finance papers include STONK (arXiv:2508.13327, DSAA 2025; daily stock direction from news sentiment plus indicators) and *Beyond Words* (SSRN, posted 2026-01-02; FOMC transcript/audio/video signals for second-level realized-volatility forecasting). CMTF (arXiv:2504.13522) appeared after CAMEF's February preprint but before its August KDD publication and predicts FTSE-100 direction from prices, macro indicators, and news. Treat these as parallel/adjacent work unless a paper explicitly cites and extends CAMEF.

- Assistant assessment: counterfactual contrastive text may improve representations and forecasts, but the objective and prediction errors alone do not establish real-world causal identification. Keep this assessment separate from the authors' causal claims.
- Code inspection (not a full runtime reproduction): `CAMEF/CAMEF/model/CAMEF.py` defines `fuse_project` at lines 74-78 as `Linear(1536,1024) -> GELU -> Linear(1024,768)`, not the hypothesized `1024 -> 1024` second layer. In `predict_batch_contrastive` (lines 230-236) and `predict_single_case` (lines 277-283), text and series tensors `[B,768]` are stacked to `[B,2,768]`, reshaped to `[B,1536]`, then passed through `fuse_project` to `[B,768]`. There is no explicit transpose or matrix multiplication; `nn.Linear` acts on the last dimension. The ordinary `forward` method (lines 110-132) does not call `fuse_project`; it concatenates two tokens to `[1,2,768]` and sends them directly through GPT-2. No custom initialization for `fuse_project` is present, so PyTorch `nn.Linear` defaults apply.
- Causal/counterfactual training mapping: there is no direct loss between a counterfactual forecast and observed ground-truth prices. In `model/CAMEF.py`, forecast `output` is compared with `batch_pred_scale` using MSE and MAE (lines 595-598). Separately, `contrastive_loss_objective` (lines 439-480) uses the historical-series embedding as anchor, the factual announcement inserted at index 0 as the positive, and generated same-type counterfactual plus different-type reports as negatives; the active returned term is triplet loss. The three terms are summed at line 608 and backpropagated at line 616. Thus counterfactuals supervise representation geometry, while observed future prices supervise only the factual forecast. `data/dataloader.py` lines 152-156 constructs the ground truth from the post-event series window.
- Local implementation map (inspected 2026-10-05): `CAMEF/CAMEF/train.py` and `test.py` are CLI entry points; `data/dataloader.py` discovers event text, pairs it with pre/post-event series windows, samples same-type counterfactual and other-type negative reports, fits a train-only `StandardScaler`, and creates 6:2:2 loaders; `model/CAMEF.py` contains all encoders, fusion/regression layers, losses, training, evaluation, checkpointing, and sample export; `moment.yml` is a Linux/CUDA Conda environment lock; both `__init__.py` files only mark packages.
- Code-review cautions (not runtime-tested): default splitting is by filesystem/index order unless `--split-by-time` is passed; training evaluates the test loader every epoch; `test()` switches the model to eval mode and `train()` does not visibly restore `model.train()` in later epochs; target tensors in training are moved to hard-coded `cuda`, so CPU-only training can fail; gradient clipping occurs after `optimizer.step()`; `test_save` assumes exactly four series channels; event type extraction uses `event_folder.split('/')[2]`, which is not portable to Windows paths. Report discovery occurs inside a single date folder, so it does match the current event; the earlier cross-date concern was incorrect. These are implementation risks, not paper claims.
- Dataset audit (read-only, 2026-10-05): actual bundle is `dataset/events/processed_events_and_counterfactuals/{1..6}/<YYYYMMDD>.txt/` (2,147 event directories, each with one nonempty full summary and 10 valid `report_sent` files) plus `dataset/time-sersseries/` (INDU, NASDAQ, RTY, SP500, USGG1M, USGG5YR CSVs; an Apple `._SP500.csv` sidecar should be ignored). Some event folders additionally contain `[Wrong]` reports, excluded by the loader's exact `report_sent{i}.txt` glob. All six CSVs parse with required date/date_int/OHLC columns, no NA or invalid OHLC ordering; date and date_int agree. `INDU.csv` has 20,304 exact duplicate timestamp/rows; `SP500.csv` has 162 and two backward timestamp jumps. Concrete suspicious prices: INDU 2013-07-09 10:50 has Low=0, Close=19.34 amid ~15,270 neighbors; SP500 2010-01-11 16:10 has Low=Close=1047.28 amid ~1147/1144 neighboring bars. These rows should be checked against source before correction. USGG1M has nonpositive yields, which are not automatically errors.
- Dataset/code integration: actual paths differ from `train.py` defaults (`data/event`, `data/series`), and `event_set` expects exactly the processed event root plus the time-series CSV directory. On Windows, `event_folder.split('/')[2]` can misidentify event type or raise IndexError even with correct paths; use `pathlib`/`os.path` if implementing a fix. `event_id==4` appends seven digits `1400005` to eight-digit date rather than a six-digit HHMMSS stamp; other IDs append `083000` to every event type. This timing logic requires verification before valid pairing. No model training was run during audit.
- MOMENT training distinction (verified 2026-10-05): CAMEF paper Appendix C.2 explicitly claims a first stage pre-training MOMENT on time-series segments from the CAMEF training set, then a multimodal stage freezing all MOMENT parameters; it does not specify first-stage loss, mask rate, epochs, checkpoint, or reproducible commands. The released local code does not implement that first stage: `train.py` defaults to `AutonLab/MOMENT-1-large`; `model/CAMEF.py` calls `MOMENTPipeline.from_pretrained(..., task_name='embedding')` and `init()`, then `train()` sets all MOMENT parameters `requires_grad=False`. MOMENT parameters are listed in Adam but frozen parameters receive no gradients/updates. `embed_project`, `residual_project`, fusion and output layers train on CAMEF. The original MOMENT paper/repo describe masked patch reconstruction on the broad Time-series Pile as its own pre-training mechanism: split series into patches, mask random patches, reconstruct series using a lightweight head. Do not claim CAMEF's additional stage used exactly that objective without code/details. Sources: CAMEF arXiv v3 Appendix C.2 (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04592v3); MOMENT ICML 2024 paper (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/goswami24a.html) and official repo (https://github.com/moment-timeseries-foundation-model/moment).
